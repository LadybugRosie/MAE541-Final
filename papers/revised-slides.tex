\section{Introductory Remarks}

\begin{frame}{Motivation: Brain Rhythms, Dimensionality, and Seizure-Like Synchrony}
\small

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.57\textwidth}

\textbf{Why study rhythmic neural activity?}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Neural circuits process time-varying signals: sensory inputs,
        motor rhythms, and internal oscillations.
  \item These signals can often be understood as combinations of simpler
        oscillatory components, like a Fourier series.
  \item Good representation requires rich, flexible activity rather than
        every neuron doing the same thing.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.3em}

\textbf{What changes in seizure-like states?}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Seizures involve abnormal synchronization across neural populations.
  \item When many neurons fire together, the activity becomes less diverse.
  \item In linear-algebra terms, this resembles a collapse in effective
        dimensionality.
\end{itemize}

\end{column}

\begin{column}{0.40\textwidth}

\begin{block}{Central Question}
Does Oja-type plasticity under rhythmic input preserve high-dimensional
activity, or drive the network toward low-dimensional synchrony?
\end{block}

\vspace{0.4em}

\textbf{Project idea:}

We study whether a simple local learning rule can reshape the stability of
a recurrent network and create a pathway toward synchronized dynamics.

\end{column}
\end{columns}

\end{frame}
\begin{frame}{Background: Fixed-Weight Recurrent Networks}
\small

A common starting point is a recurrent neural network whose synaptic weights
are fixed in time:

\[
\tau \dot{x}_i
=
-x_i
+
f\left(\sum_j W_{ij}x_j+b_i\right).
\]

Here \(x_i(t)\) is the activity of neuron \(i\), \(W_{ij}\) is the weight from
neuron \(j\) to neuron \(i\), \(b_i\) is an external input or bias, and \(f\)
is a nonlinear activation function.

\vspace{0.5em}

In this setting, memory and computation are usually studied through the
evolving neural state \({\bf x}(t)\), while the matrix \(W\) defines the fixed
recurrent architecture.

\vspace{0.5em}

\begin{block}{Dynamical systems viewpoint}
For fixed \(W\), stability, oscillations, and chaos are controlled by the
interaction between the weight spectrum and the nonlinear activity dynamics.
\end{block}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Background: The Grossberg \(S\)-\(\Sigma\) Viewpoint}
A recurrent rate model is often written in the form

\[
\tau \dot{x}_i
=
-x_i
+
f\left(\sum_j W_{ij}x_j+b_i\right).
\]

In this formulation, the nonlinear activation is applied after neuron \(i\)
integrates its total recurrent input.

\vspace{0.7em}

An alternative formulation, common in Grossberg-type models, is

\[
\tau \dot{x}_i
=
-x_i
+
\sum_j W_{ij}S(x_j)
+
b_i.
\]

Here \(S(x_j)\) is interpreted as the nonlinear signal emitted by the
presynaptic neuron \(j\), which is then weighted by \(W_{ij}\) and summed by
the postsynaptic neuron.

\vspace{0.7em}

\begin{block}{Formal interpretation}
The \(S\)-\(\Sigma\) viewpoint separates the internal activity variable
\(x_j\) from the transmitted signal \(S(x_j)\). Thus, the nonlinearity can be
represented either as a postsynaptic activation applied to the integrated
input, or as a presynaptic signal function applied before synaptic summation.
\end{block}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Background: Learning in Recurrent Neural Networks}
\small

In the fixed-weight setting, the state \({\bf x}(t)\) evolves through a fixed recurrent
architecture \(W\). Introducing learning makes the weights dynamic, so the
system evolves in the enlarged state space

\[
({\bf x}(t),W(t)).
\]

\vspace{0.5em}

A standard local model of synaptic modification is Hebbian learning,

\[
\dot W_{ij}
=
\eta x_i x_j,
\]

which increases the weight \(W_{ij}\) when the presynaptic and postsynaptic
activities are correlated. However, this rule alone can lead to unbounded
growth of the weights.

\vspace{0.5em}

Oja's rule modifies Hebbian learning by adding an activity-dependent
normalization term:

\[
\dot W_{ij}
=
\eta x_i x_j
-
\eta x_i^2 W_{ij}.
\]

\vspace{0.4em}

Oja-type plasticity preserves the local Hebbian dependence on neural activity
while keeping the weights bounded. As a result, the recurrent architecture
\(W(t)\) evolves with the neural state and can change the stability and
long-term behavior of the network.

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Background: Girko's Circular Law}
\small

Let \(W^{(n)} = (W_{ij}) \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}\) be a random matrix whose entries are
independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) real random variables satisfying
\[
\mathbb{E}[W_{ij}] = 0,
\qquad
\mathbb{E}[W_{ij}^2] = \frac{g^2}{n},
\qquad
\mathbb{E}[|W_{ij}|^{2+\epsilon}] < \infty.
\]

Let \(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n \in \mathbb{C}\) denote the eigenvalues of \(W^{(n)}\), and define the empirical spectral measure
\[
\mu_n
=
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^n \delta_{\lambda_k}.
\]

Then Girko's circular law states that, as \(n \to \infty\),
\[
\mu_n \;\Rightarrow\; \mu_{\mathrm{circ}},
\]
where \(\Rightarrow\) denotes weak convergence of probability measures and
\[
d\mu_{\mathrm{circ}}(z)
=
\frac{1}{\pi g^2}\mathbf{1}_{\{|z|\le g\}}\, d^2 z
\]
is the uniform probability measure on the disk of radius \(g\) in \(\mathbb{C}\).

\vspace{0.5em}

\begin{block}{Interpretation for recurrent networks}
For large random recurrent networks, the spectrum of the weight matrix becomes asymptotically uniform over a disk in the complex plane. The parameter \(g\) controls the spectral radius and therefore the effective strength of recurrent feedback, determining whether the system is stable, oscillatory, or unstable.
\end{block}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Special Case: Internally Driven Fixed-Weight Dynamics}
\small

First consider the case with no external drive and zero bias:

\[
\tau \dot{x}
=
-x+\tanh(Wx).
\]

All activity is generated internally by the recurrent network. Since
\(\tanh(0)=0\), the origin is an equilibrium:

\[
x^\ast=0.
\]

Linearizing at \(x=0\), using \(\tanh'(0)=1\), gives

\[
J(0)=\frac{1}{\tau}(-I+W).
\]

If \(W_{ij}\) are random with

\[
\mathbb{E}[W_{ij}]=0,
\qquad
\mathrm{Var}(W_{ij})=\frac{g^2}{n},
\]

then Girko's circular law implies that the eigenvalues of \(W\) approximately
fill the disk \(|z|\leq g\).

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Consequence: Local Stability Threshold}
\small

The linearized dynamics near \(x=0\) are determined by

\[
J(0)=\frac{1}{\tau}(-I+W).
\]

Thus each eigenvalue \(\lambda\) of \(W\) corresponds to a Jacobian eigenvalue

\[
\mu
=
\frac{\lambda-1}{\tau}.
\]

By Girko's circular law, the eigenvalues of \(W\) approximately fill the disk

\[
|\lambda|\leq g.
\]

Therefore, the Jacobian eigenvalues approximately fill the shifted and rescaled disk

\[
\left|\tau \mu + 1\right| \leq g.
\]

The rightmost point of this disk is

\[
\mu_{\max}
\approx
\frac{g-1}{\tau}.
\]

If \(g<1\), all Jacobian eigenvalues have negative real part, so the internal
activity decays back to \(x=0\). If \(g>1\), some directions become locally
unstable, so small perturbations can be amplified by recurrent feedback.

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Heuristic: Oja Learning Tracks Activity Correlations}
\small

For fixed activity \(x\), the Oja-type rule

\[
\dot W_{ij}
=
\eta x_i(x_j-x_iW_{ij})-\lambda W_{ij}
\]

has equilibrium

\[
W_{ij}^{\ast}
=
\frac{\eta x_i x_j}{\eta x_i^2+\lambda}.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
W^\ast = u x^T,
\qquad
u_i=
\frac{\eta x_i}{\eta x_i^2+\lambda}.
\]

Thus, for constant activity, the learned matrix is rank at most one. This
does not require the approximation
\(\eta x_i^2 \ll \lambda\).

\vspace{0.5em}

For time-dependent driven activity, \(x(t)\), the analogous heuristic is

\[
W_{ij}^{\ast}
\approx
\frac{\eta \langle x_i(t)x_j(t)\rangle}
{\eta \langle x_i(t)^2\rangle+\lambda}.
\]

Heuristically, Oja plasticity tends to align \(W(t)\) with the normalized correlation
structure of the activity. Low-rank collapse is expected only if the driven

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Driven Dynamics: Linearization}
\small

With time-dependent input, the dynamics are

\[
\tau \dot{x}
=
-x+\tanh(Wx+b(t)).
\]

Around a driven trajectory \(x^\ast(t)\), the perturbation dynamics satisfy

\[
\tau \dot{\delta x}
=
\left[-I+D(t)W\right]\delta x,
\]

where

\[
D(t)
=
\operatorname{diag}\left(
\tanh'(Wx^\ast(t)+b(t))
\right)
=
\operatorname{diag}\left(
\operatorname{sech}^2(Wx^\ast(t)+b(t))
\right).
\]

Thus the effective recurrent matrix is

\[
D(t)W.
\]

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Driven Dynamics: Effective Random-Matrix Gain}
\small

If \(W_{ij}\) has variance \(g^2/n\), then TODO WHAT DOES CONDITIONALLY MEAN HERE conditionally on \(D(t)\),

\[
\mathrm{Var}\big((D(t)W)_{ij}\big)
=
\frac{g^2 d_i(t)^2}{n},
\qquad
d_i(t)=D_{ii}(t).
\]

This suggests the heuristic effective gain

\[
g_{\mathrm{eff}}(t)
\approx
g
\sqrt{
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n d_i(t)^2
}.
\]

Girko's law gives \(g=1\) as the undriven fixed-weight reference threshold.
In the driven nonlinear system, saturation changes the effective gain through
\(D(t)\), so instability is better tracked by \(g_{\mathrm{eff}}(t)\) than by
\(g\) alone.

\end{frame}



\section{Numerical Results}

\begin{frame}{Oja Learning Does Not Self-Organize to the Edge of Chaos}
\small

A key motivation for studying Oja-type plasticity in recurrent networks is the
conjecture that local learning rules might spontaneously drive the network
toward a critical operating point near the edge of chaos, where $\rho(W) \approx 1$.

\vspace{0.4em}

To test this, we sweep initial spectral radii
$g_0 \in \{0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5\}$, initialize
$W_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, g_0^2/n)$, and run the full coupled system

\[
\tau \dot{x}_i = -x_i + \tanh\!\Bigl(\textstyle\sum_j W_{ij} x_j
    + A\sin(\omega t + \phi_i)\Bigr),
\qquad
\dot{W}_{ij} = \eta x_i(x_j - x_i W_{ij}) - \lambda W_{ij},
\]

with $A=1$ (driven) and $A=0$ (autonomous) for $T=400$ time units.

\vspace{0.4em}

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.57\textwidth}
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../oja_chaos/spectral_radius_trajectories.png}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.40\textwidth}
\begin{block}{Finding}
Under sinusoidal drive, $\rho(W) \to 45$ for every initial $g_0$.
The drive erases all memory of the initial condition.
The autonomous system ($A=0$) does exhibit a clean bifurcation at $g_0=1$, but
the addition of periodic input \emph{unfolds} this bifurcation, removing
the stable branch near $\rho(W)=1$.
\end{block}
\end{column}
\end{columns}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Why the Drive Causes Runaway Growth}
\small

\textbf{Separation of timescales.} Activity relaxes on $\tau=1$; weights evolve
on $1/\eta=100$; since $\eta\tau=0.01\ll 1$, $W$ is approximately constant on
the fast (activity) timescale. By the averaging method we hold $W$ fixed and
study the fast $x$-subsystem. \emph{Relaxes} means transients from initial
conditions decay exponentially at rate $(1-\rho(DW))/\tau$, leaving only the
driven response. For $g<1$, $\rho(DW)\leq\rho(W)<1$ (since $d_i\leq 1$), so
the Poincar\'{e} map is a contraction and by the Banach fixed-point theorem
there is a \emph{unique} globally attracting periodic orbit at frequency
$\omega$; for $g>1$ the argument is heuristic. Averaging over one drive period
$T_{\mathrm{avg}}=2\pi/\omega$ gives the correlation matrix felt by the slow
$W$ dynamics:

\[
C(W) = \frac{1}{T_{\mathrm{avg}}}\int_0^{T_{\mathrm{avg}}} x(t;W)\,x(t;W)^T \, dt.
\]

The weight dynamics on the slow manifold reduce to

\[
\dot{W}_{ij} \approx
\eta\bigl[C_{ij}(W) - C_{ii}(W)\, W_{ij}\bigr] - \lambda W_{ij}.
\]

Setting this to zero gives the fixed point

\[
W_{ij}^\ast = \frac{\eta\, C_{ij}(W^\ast)}{\eta\, C_{ii}(W^\ast) + \lambda}.
\]

\vspace{0.3em}

Under sinusoidal drive at frequency $\omega$, every neuron responds at the same frequency,
so that approximately $x_i(t) \approx r_i \sin(\omega t + \theta_i)$.
The time-averaged correlation matrix is then

\[
C_{ij}(W) \approx \tfrac{1}{2} r_i r_j \cos(\theta_i - \theta_j),
\]

which has rank at most one. Consequently $W^\ast \approx \frac{\eta}{\lambda} r r^T$
(up to diagonal factors), and the spectral radius satisfies
$\rho(W^\ast) \approx \frac{\eta}{\lambda}\|r\|^2$. Since $\|r\|$ itself grows with
$\rho(W)$, this is a self-reinforcing feedback loop with no finite
fixed point under saturation-limited activity.

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Edge-of-Chaos Optimality for Signal Reconstruction}
\small

Although Oja learning does not maintain the network at the edge of chaos,
the initial spectral radius $g$ still determines reconstruction quality.

\vspace{0.3em}

For each $g$, we run the driven Oja system for $T=80$ time units and fit a
linear readout $c^\ast \in \mathbb{R}^n$ to reconstruct the target signal
$y(t) = \sin(\omega t)$ from the activity history $X \in \mathbb{R}^{T/dt \times n}$:

\[
c^\ast = \arg\min_{c}\,\|Xc - y\|^2,
\qquad
R^2 = 1 - \frac{\|Xc^\ast - y\|^2}{\mathrm{Var}(y)}.
\]

The window $T=80$ is the natural measurement regime: transients have decayed
(since $\tau=1$) but Oja has not yet fully saturated the network
($\rho(W) \approx 10$--$20$).

\vspace{0.3em}

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.53\textwidth}
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../oja_chaos/r2_vs_g.png}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.44\textwidth}
\begin{block}{Finding}
$R^2$ peaks at $g \approx 0.9$--$1.0$, reaching
$R^2_{\max} \approx 0.81 \pm 0.06$, and falls to
$R^2 \approx 0.22$ at $g = 3.0$.
Networks initialized at the edge of chaos produce the richest activity during
the learning transient, enabling more accurate reconstruction before Oja saturation
degrades the signal.
\end{block}

\begin{frame}{Frozen Random Reservoir Outperforms Oja-Learned Weights}
\small

To assess the value of Oja learning directly, we compare to a frozen baseline.
For each $g$, we run the same driven network with weights held fixed ($\eta = 0$)
and fit a ridge-regression readout with regularization $\alpha = 10^{-3}$:

\[
c^\ast = (X^TX + \alpha I)^{-1}X^T y.
\]

\vspace{0.3em}

\begin{column}{0.54\textwidth}
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../network_analysis/r2_baseline_vs_oja.png}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.43\textwidth}
\vspace{0.2em}
The frozen reservoir achieves $R^2 \approx 1.0$ for every $g$.
This is not a trivial result: even a static random matrix, when driven by
$A\sin(\omega t)$, projects the input into a high-dimensional space from which
the target frequency can always be recovered by a linear readout.

\vspace{0.4em}

\begin{block}{Finding}
Oja learning is counterproductive. It drives $\rho(W) \to 45$,
saturating the $\tanh$ nonlinearity and destroying the activity diversity
that makes reconstruction easy. The frozen network retains this diversity.
\end{block}
\end{column}
\end{columns}

\begin{frame}{Input Structure Drives Rank Collapse: A Mechanistic Test}
\small

The slow-manifold analysis predicts that rank collapse is tied to the rank of
$C(W)$, not to the learning rule per se. We test this by replacing the sinusoidal
drive with three alternatives:

\vspace{0.2em}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Quasi-periodic:}
      $A/\sqrt{3}\sum_{k=1}^3 \sin(\omega_k t + \phi_i)$,
      with $\omega_1=1$, $\omega_2=\varphi$ (golden ratio), $\omega_3=\sqrt{2}$.
\item \emph{White noise:} $\xi_i(t) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, A^2/2)$ independently each step.
\item \emph{Zero drive:} $A=0$ (autonomous network).
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.3em}

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.56\textwidth}
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../signal_comparison/correlation_rank_by_drive.png}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.41\textwidth}
\begin{block}{Finding}
For $g < 1$ (stable regime), white noise keeps $\mathrm{rank}(\hat{C}) \approx n$
and prevents rank collapse entirely: $\rho(W)\to 0$, $\mathrm{PR}(W)\approx 36$.
Sinusoidal drive collapses $\mathrm{rank}(\hat{C})\to 1$ within $T\approx 50$,
and $\rho(W)\to 43$ in all cases.

For $g > 1.5$ (chaotic regime), even white noise causes collapse, because the
dominant Lyapunov mode of the chaotic network creates its own rank-one correlation
structure that Oja then amplifies.
\end{block}
\end{column}
\end{columns}

\end{frame}

The heuristic $g_{\mathrm{eff}}(t)\approx(\|W(t)\|_F/\sqrt{n})\sqrt{\mathrm{mean}(d_i^2)}$
was tested in two ways: tracking it during Oja learning, and scanning frozen-weight
networks across $g$.

\end{block}

\begin{frame}{Critical Slowing Down at the Phase Transition}
\small

Independent evidence for the $g = 1$ phase transition comes from measuring the
relaxation time of the autonomous discrete map $h_{t+1} = W f(h_t)$.

\vspace{0.3em}

A small perturbation $\delta h(0)$ evolves as $\|\delta h(t)\| \sim \|\delta h(0)\| e^{\lambda_1 t}$
in the linear regime, where $\lambda_1$ is the largest Lyapunov exponent. For $\lambda_1 < 0$
the relaxation time is $\tau_{\mathrm{relax}} = -1/\lambda_1$; for $\lambda_1 > 0$ the
perturbation grows and $\tau_{\mathrm{relax}}$ is undefined.

\vspace{0.3em}

\section{Concluding Remarks}

\begin{frame}{Summary}
\small

We have studied a recurrent network in which both activity and weights evolve
simultaneously under Oja-type plasticity and sinusoidal drive. The main findings are:

\vspace{0.3em}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Oja learning \emph{does not} self-organize the spectral radius toward
      the edge of chaos. Under sinusoidal drive, $\rho(W) \to 45$ regardless
      of the initial $g_0$, because the drive creates a persistent rank-one
      correlation structure that Oja amplifies without bound.

\item The initial spectral radius $g$ still determines signal reconstruction
      quality: $R^2$ peaks near $g=1$, where network activity is richest during
      the early learning transient.

\item A frozen random reservoir with ridge regression outperforms
      the Oja-learned network for all $g$, because Oja learning destroys
      the activity diversity that makes reconstruction tractable.

\item Rank collapse requires \emph{structured} periodic input in the stable regime
      ($g < 1$): white noise drives do not collapse $\rho(W)$ or reduce the
      participation ratio. In the chaotic regime ($g > 1.5$), collapse occurs
      regardless of input structure due to the dominant Lyapunov mode.

\item Critical slowing down at $g=1$ — $\tau_{\mathrm{relax}} \to \infty$ —
      provides a physically observable signature of the phase transition that
      is independent of the Lyapunov exponent sign.
\end{enumerate}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Open Questions}
\small

Several questions remain for future work.

\vspace{0.4em}

The fixed-point analysis shows that $W^\ast \approx \frac{\eta}{\lambda}C(W^\ast)$
on the slow manifold. A full stability analysis of this self-consistent equation
would clarify whether the $\rho(W)\to\infty$ trajectory is the only attractor
under sinusoidal drive, or whether there exist bounded fixed points at large $n$.

\vspace{0.4em}

The quasi-periodic drive collapses $\rho(W)$ as strongly as the single sinusoid,
which suggests the number of active modes in $C$ is less important than whether
any structured periodic components are present. A precise criterion —
characterizing exactly which input power spectra lead to rank collapse — would
sharpen the connection to the seizure hypothesis.

\vspace{0.4em}

\begin{block}{Seizure connection}
The key experimental prediction is: rhythmic, periodic input is necessary
for Oja-driven rank collapse in stable networks, while random input is
not sufficient. This suggests that pathological periodicity — rather than
high firing rates alone — may be the mechanistically important feature
of seizure-like activity driving network-level dimensionality collapse.
\end{block}

\end{frame}
